## The one thing this block is built around

Every system you train with — the **Recovery Gauge**, RPE ceilings, "add load only if it felt like RPE 7 or below" — runs on you accurately reading your own body.

**Jet lag is precisely the thing that breaks that reading.** For the next few days your perceived effort, your motivation, your appetite and your sense of how heavy a bar is are all being generated by a clock that still thinks it's in Sweden. A gauge of 2 today tells you about your circadian phase, not your recovery. An RPE 7 today is not the same physical load as an RPE 7 next week.

So this week the plan **takes the decisions off you.** Loads are written in kilograms, not percentages of feel. Session times are set by the clock, not by readiness. The Recovery Gauge is suspended until Sep 5.

That's the fix. Not more discipline — fewer judgement calls, made in advance, by someone who isn't jet-lagged.

## Part 1 — The clock (this is the actual training this week)

Six hours eastward. Eastward is the hard direction: you're asking your body to fall asleep when it thinks it's late afternoon. Rough rate is about a day per hour, so you're looking at **aligned around Sep 3–4** — which is why the Seoul flight is good news, not bad:

> **Seoul is UTC+9. Hong Kong is UTC+8. That's one hour. It is not a second jet-lag event.**
> You are doing *one* adaptation, and you're already halfway through it. Nothing about Sep 5 restarts this.

### The light rule — read this one properly, it's counterintuitive

"Get morning light" is the standard advice and **for the first days of an eastward shift it is actively wrong.**

Light works relative to your body's temperature minimum (Tmin) — roughly two hours before your habitual wake time. Light *after* Tmin pulls your clock **earlier** (what you want). Light *before* Tmin pushes it **later** (the wrong way).

You arrived on a Swedish clock, waking ~07:00 CEST. That puts your Tmin at roughly **11:00 Hong Kong time on arrival day**. Standing in bright Hong Kong sun at 08:00 that morning would have pushed you further from where you're going.

Three days in, your Tmin has advanced. Estimated schedule — **walk the light window earlier each day:**

| Date | Get outdoor light from | Keep it dim before | Lights-out target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Aug 31 (today)** | 10:00 | 09:00 | 00:30 |
| Sep 1 | 09:00 | 08:00 | 00:00 |
| Sep 2 | 08:00 | 07:00 | 23:30 |
| Sep 3 | 07:00 | — | 23:00 |
| Sep 4 onward | on waking | — | 23:00 |

Outdoor light, not through a window — an overcast Hong Kong sky beats any indoor room by an order of magnitude. Sunglasses on if you're out before the window opens.

*(Tmin is estimated from your travel, not measured. If you're falling asleep fine but waking at 04:00, you've over-advanced — hold the window where it is for a day instead of moving it earlier.)*

### The rest of the levers

- **Caffeine: Hong Kong morning only, nothing after 12:00.** Through Sep 3.
- **Naps: 20 minutes, never after 15:00.** If you're wrecked at 16:00, go outside and walk instead. Walking in daylight does two jobs at once.
- **Meal timing is a circadian cue, not just food.** Eat on Hong Kong meal times even when you're not hungry — a real protein-forward breakfast in the Hong Kong morning genuinely helps pull the clock earlier. Skipping breakfast because you're "not hungry yet" is your Swedish stomach voting, and it slows the shift.
- **Wind-Down is the nightly default,** same as the Sweden block. [`wind-down-bailout.md`](./wind-down-bailout.md), 1–2 h before lights-out. On the nights you don't train it *is* the session.

## Part 2 — Hong Kong is not Sweden, and the running plan doesn't transfer

The Sweden block was aerobic-led for a specific reason: 18–22 °C, dry, long light. That was the whole justification, and it was a good one.

**Hong Kong at the end of August is 30 °C and humid.** It is the same swamp you were escaping in Tokyo. Running here now stacks heat strain on top of circadian strain, on legs that have run five times since May, with calves you've flagged as your injury risk since March.

So the aerobic work drops to **one easy run** this week, and **walking becomes the main aerobic lever.** That isn't a downgrade — in this heat and this state, an hour of walking is the better session, and it's also the light exposure and the step count. Three things, one activity.

**Running comes back properly in Seoul.** Low-20s, dry, and the Han River paths are flat and endless. That's where the base you started building in Sweden gets picked up again.

If you do run here: **before 07:30 or after 19:30, nothing in between.** **Bowen Road** is the one — flat, shaded, about 4 km out, straight up from Happy Valley. Conversational pace, and in this humidity conversational will be much slower than your ego expects. That's the heat, not your fitness.

**Wong Nai Chung Gap and the climbs are parked.** Not forever, and not because they're too hard — because a hill session in 30 °C, mid-jet-lag, on legs that have run five times since May is structurally the same mistake as Session E on Aug 25: a hard session bought before the base can hold it. Same kind of unlock condition, written now so it isn't a mood call later: **two weeks of consistent easy running with no calf or shin complaints.**

## Part 3 — Getting back on a bar

### The base

**HKFC gym, 07:00–22:00. Barbell and rack.** That settles it — the written loads below stand exactly as they are, no dumbbell substitutions needed.

Fifteen hours of daily access removes the last excuse the travel blocks had, and it means the *only* thing choosing your session time is your body clock. Which is why Part 3 ends with a time of day rather than a readiness score.

**No pitch and no treadmill.** So the Road Block's field sessions — Crossings, The Grid — have nowhere to run and are retired for this block. No loss: you have a barbell now, which is strictly better for what you're trying to do.

### Why a reintroduction and not a test

Last confirmed barbell session: **2026-05-20.** That's fourteen weeks.

The old file's answer to this was a recalibration week starting Aug 30. **That's cancelled** — see *The baseline* below. What happens instead is three reintroduction sessions at loads chosen in advance, which you do not get to adjust upward on the day.

### Why the numbers look insultingly light

After fourteen weeks off, the thing that limits you is not muscle — it's tendon, connective tissue and pattern, and those come back slower than strength does. The first session back near old working weights buys you four days of DOMS, and you have a flight on Sep 5.

There is also nothing to gain. A heavy day this week cannot make you stronger by Seoul. It can only make the next three days worse.

**Session 1 is 50% of the May working weight. Session 2 is 60%. Session 3 is 70%.** Written out below so there's no arithmetic in the gym.

| Bench Press | 3 × 8 | **40 kg** | **56 kg** |

### Re-Entry B — full body, hinge/press-led

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | **Sess 2 (Sep 2)** |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Hanging Knee Raise *or* Plank | 3 × 30–45 s | — |

**Rules for all three:**

- **45 minutes, hard cap.** Walk out at 45 even mid-set.
- **The written weight is the weight.** Not a minimum. If it flies up, good — that's the session working as designed, and you log "felt light" and take the next number next time.
- **No top sets. No AMRAPs. No testing anything.** Not one.
- **Full range, slow eccentrics.** These sessions are pattern work. Load is almost incidental.
- **Calves are not optional.** Same reason as always.

### When to train — the non-obvious bit

Your force output tracks your core temperature, and your core temperature is still on Swedish time. Right now the Hong Kong morning is your subjective night, and the Hong Kong evening is your subjective afternoon.

**So this week, train late: 16:00–19:00.** You will feel meaningfully stronger then than at 09:00, and it costs you nothing because your light window is in the morning and you're walking for that anyway. As you align, the session naturally drifts earlier. By Seoul, train whenever suits.

**The session-time rule, so there's nothing to negotiate:** if it's before 19:30, the gym session happens. If the window's gone, it's Wind-Down instead, and that's a completed day.

One hour east. No new adaptation. What changes is the weather and the terrain, both in your favour.

- **Han River paths** — flat, lit, endless, and the single best place to rebuild aerobic base in that city. This is where the Sweden work gets picked back up.
- **Namsan** — stairs and hills when you want them. Not yet.

**Rotation from Sep 7,** three gym days plus aerobic:

**Upper A** → easy run → **Lower** → walk → **Upper B** → long easy run → rest

Loads continue from the Sep 4 number, adding conservatively. Same 45-minute cap. RPE ceiling comes back to 7, and the **Recovery Gauge comes back into force from Sep 5** — by then it's measuring something real again.

## Part 6 — The baseline (deferred, on a condition)

The recalibration week was scheduled for Aug 30 and did not happen, because on Aug 30 you were 24 hours off a red-eye.

**It is not being rescheduled to a date. It runs on a condition:**

> **Three consecutive nights of normal sleep in one timezone, *and* two gym sessions already logged in that timezone.**

Realistically that lands around **Sep 9–11, in Seoul.** It might land later. That's fine — nothing in the plan depends on it happening this month.

**Why not sooner:** eastward travel measurably suppresses maximal force output for several days after the subjective jet lag has faded. A baseline taken this week wouldn't measure you, it'd measure the flight — and it would hand you a low anchor to build the next block on, which is worse than having no number at all.

**The Aug 25 reframe still stands and matters more now:** this is a **baseline, not a test.** One top set per lift at **RPE 7 — a rep left in the tank, no grinding.** The number gets estimated from that, not proven. You're coming off fourteen weeks off a bar and four countries in three weeks; a max attempt in that state measures your summer, not your ceiling. Take the RPE 7 number, build on it, let the ceiling turn up on its own in six weeks.
