## Part 1 — Is 100 kg realistic? Yes.

You are not building a 100 kg bench. You are **taking one back.**

| Date | Evidence | Implied 1RM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-03-23 | Bench **90 kg × 8** | ~110–113 kg |
| 2026-05-20 | Bench **80 kg × 8 @ RPE 6**, 3 sets | ~100–105 kg |
| 2026-09-12 | Bench **80 kg × 1** | ~80–82 kg |

A ~25 kg drop across a four-month layoff, two continents, and a jet-lag block whose heaviest prescribed bench was **56 kg**. None of that is tissue loss. Four months does not delete the muscle that pressed 90 × 8 in March — it deletes **the pattern**: bar path, brace, leg drive, the nervous system's willingness to stay tight under a load it hasn't seen since spring.

Re-acquiring a pattern runs roughly three times faster than building it the first time. The required rate is **+1.5 kg of 1RM per week.** For new strength at your training age that's fantasy. For reclaiming a number you owned six months ago it's the normal curve — and most of it lands in the first five weeks, not spread evenly.

At 81 kg bodyweight, 100 kg is **1.23 × bodyweight.** You've been there.

### The one condition, and it isn't physiological

Your file records **four programme collapses** — April, May, late May, August. Same shape every time: high-ceiling structure → school or travel or mood → base loss → demoralising re-entry. This ask is where instance five gets written.

What decides 100 kg is not the percentages below. It's whether you average **2.5 lifting sessions a week for thirteen weeks** — one full semester, and you haven't strung four consistent weeks together since March.

**Week 4 is the checkpoint, and it isn't pass/fail:**

- ≥ 2.5 sessions/wk → 100 kg stays.
- 2 → target becomes **95 kg.** Same plan. Nothing abandoned.
- < 2 → the problem isn't the programme and we stop redesigning it.

### What actually caps you at 95

Not your chest. Three things:

1. **Overhead press** — machine 8–12 kg against a 90 kg bench is the widest gap in your file. A weak press is a poor shoulder platform, and a poor platform limits how hard you're *willing* to push near max.
2. **Lockout** — benches in the 90–100 range fail 4–6 inches off the chest. Triceps. Trainable in eight weeks.
3. **Upper back** — you press off your back, not off the bench. Assisted-only pull-ups at max 5 is the structural gap flagged since March.

## Part 2 — Glutes: what "definition" actually needs

Two separate things, and only one of them is trainable right now.

**Shape** comes from muscle — gluteus maximus for the mass, and **gluteus medius for the upper-outer shelf** that most people never train and that does most of the visual work. That's this block.

**Definition** comes from leanness, and **you are not cutting until after 13 December** (see Part 5 — you cannot reclaim 20 kg of bench in a deficit). So the honest sequencing is: **this block builds it, January reveals it.** Building now and cutting in January is the correct order anyway — cutting first would just make you leaner over less.

Three things drive glute growth and you're currently doing none of them:

- **Hip thrust** — the only exercise that loads the glute hard at full hip extension, which is where it's strongest and where squats give it nothing. This is the primary lift of the day and it gets the first slot.
- **Depth** — a squat is only a glute exercise below parallel. Quarter squats build quads and ego.
- **Abduction** — glute medius. Cheap, fast-responding, and the difference between "big legs" and shape.

## Part 3 — Track: why this will feel good, and why August didn't

You ran 800/1500/3000/5k as a junior. That engine is still in there, and the **motor pattern** — turnover, relaxed speed, knowing what a lap feels like — never fully leaves. That's why running is the right ego lever for you and lifting isn't.

**But read this before you write the session yourself.** On 2026-08-25 you did The Grid — repeated 40 s / 20 s bouts — got through half of it, and came away rating yourself unfit. Your diagnosis was right, your verdict was wrong. 40/20 repeats are **lactate-tolerance work**: they depend on aerobic clearance between bouts, and your base was three months thin. Degradation across laps was expected physiology, not a character flaw.

**Short reps with full recovery are the opposite lever.** 200 m walk-back-200 m is powered by phosphocreatine, which refills in 2–3 minutes regardless of how good your aerobic base is. So you run every rep fast, every rep feels like the runner you were, and it costs you almost nothing in recovery — which matters because it's sharing a body with a max-strength block.

**That is the whole design.** Fast stuff with long rest (the ego), one easy aerobic run (the engine), and no threshold work until week 5.

**Binding constraints:** shin splints — weak calves, months off running. Never two run days back to back. Ache the day after a run → the next run becomes a walk, no negotiation. Session E stays parked.

Tier system from the base rebuild carries over, with **one change**:

Miss a session — **don't make it up.** Never stack two bench days back to back to catch up. Drop it and continue on schedule.

## Week 13 · The attempts (Dec 7 → Dec 13)

**Two days that matter. Bench first, track at the end of the week.**

### Tue or Wed — 100 kg

Nothing heavy in the four days before. Pick the day you're best rested.

| | Load | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Bar | 10 |
| 2 | 40 | 5 |
| 3 | 60 | 3 |
| 4 | 75 | 2 |
| 5 | 85 | 1 |
| 6 | 92.5 | 1 |
| 7 | 97.5 | 1 |
| 8 | **100** | **1** |

**3–4 min between singles.** Warm-up singles aren't where you prove anything — they're where you find the groove.

- **Have a spotter.** Non-negotiable at 100. No spotter, no attempt — reschedule.
- **Read 92.5.** Fast and clean → go 97.5 → 100. Grinds → take 97.5 and **stop.** 80 → 97.5 in thirteen weeks is a complete success, and the last 2.5 kg costs three weeks, not another session.
- **Two attempts at 100, maximum.** Third attempts at a true max are injury, not effort.
- **Film the top two singles.** Bar path on a near-max tells you more than the outcome does.

### Sat — 1500 m

The second number. Full warm-up, 15 min, including 4 × 100 m strides. Then run it.

**Go out on pace, not on feeling.** Every 1500 anyone has ever run too fast was lost in the first 400. Split it even and pass people in the last lap — it's the best feeling in the sport and it's the entire reason this session is in the block.

## Part 5 — The three things outside the gym that decide all of this

**1 · You are not cutting.** Mini-cut stays parked until after 13 December. You cannot reclaim 20 kg of bench, add glute mass, and run track in a deficit — all three want fuel, and so does the connective tissue that has to catch up to the muscle. **~2,800 kcal, protein floor 175 g.** Let bodyweight drift to 83–84 kg and don't flinch — that's mostly useful mass, it's leverage on a bench, and glutes are the last thing that grows in a deficit. **Body comp is a January problem, and January is when the definition shows up.**

**2 · Sleep is the actual programme.** This was requested at **03:40 on a Monday.** Max strength is the most sleep-sensitive quality there is — a near-max bench is a nervous-system output, and a nervous system on 5 hours doesn't produce one. Fixed wake time, seven days, ten minutes of outdoor light attached to it. That was already the one non-negotiable of the base rebuild. Here it's also the difference between 95 and 100.

**3 · The floor is two sessions.** A + B. Everything else is upside.
