## The stretching — what actually works, and when

Two different problems, two different tools:

- **Soreness** is inflammation and fluid sitting in the tissue. Static stretching does not drain it. **Muscle contraction does** — pumping blood through pushes it out.
- **Tightness** — actual lost range — is what static holds fix, and they work best when you're warm and not about to lift. A rest day is the ideal day for them.

### Sometime today — 6 min

Flow through it. Nothing held longer than a beat. This is circulation, not flexibility. Good between study blocks.

1. **90/90 hip switches** × 10/side — glutes + adductors
2. **Half-kneeling hip-flexor rock** × 10 slow/side
3. **Bodyweight good morning** × 15 — hamstrings, pure blood flow
4. **Slow calf raises on the floor** × 20 — the single best thing for tight calves today

### Tonight — 10 min, long holds

After dinner, before bed. **90 s each, breathe out into it, don't bounce.**

| | Hold | Hits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Couch stretch** | 90 s/side | quads + hip flexor — the Bulgarians |
| **Seated figure-4 / pigeon** | 90 s/side | glutes — the hip thrusts |
| **Straddle sit, fold forward** | 2 min | adductors — the below-parallel squats |
| **Calf on a stair edge** | 60 s/side | the RDLs and calf raises — shin-splint insurance |
| **Seated forward fold** | 90 s | hamstrings |

### The one that does the most

**A 10–15 min walk.** Flat, easy, whenever — between classes, after dinner. It moves more soreness than the entire list above. Not a throwaway line; it's the actual mechanism.
